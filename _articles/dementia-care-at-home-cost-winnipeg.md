Your mom was just diagnosed. Or your dad has started leaving the stove on. And somewhere between the worry and the late-night searching, one question keeps coming back: *how are we going to pay for this?*

Most articles about home care costs never give you a number. This one does. Here's what's covered for free, what private care actually costs per week and per month, and the ways Winnipeg families bring that cost down.

## The short answer

Start with WRHA. Call the Home Care Intake Line at **204-788-8330** to get your loved one assessed. The assessment tells you whether they qualify and what public home care will cover. Private care fills the gaps and is billed by the hour. At Bonhearts, that runs roughly **$25 to $45 an hour** depending on the care needed, and every plan is built around your family.

## Step one: what WRHA home care covers, and where families hit gaps

Home care in Manitoba is arranged through your regional health authority. In Winnipeg, that's the WRHA, and every request goes through the intake line above. It's not an insured service under the Canada Health Act, so families sometimes pay for care while they wait for public services to start.

When a family calls us worried about cost, the first thing we tell them is to call WRHA and get assessed. You need to know what your loved one qualifies for before you spend a dollar on private care.

Public home care is a strong starting point, but families often top it up. The common reasons: the hours approved don't cover evenings or weekends, visits are short, or the family simply needs more help than the assessment allows. That's where private hours come in.

## What private dementia care costs: real examples

Every plan is custom, but here's the math for three common setups at $25 to $45 an hour. Monthly figures use 52 weeks ÷ 12 months.

| Care plan | Hours per week | Per week | Per month |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 days a week, 4 hours each | 8 | $200–$360 | about $867–$1,560 |
| 3 hours every day | 21 | $525–$945 | about $2,275–$4,095 |
| 8 hours every day | 56 | $1,400–$2,520 | about $6,067–$10,920 |

Where you land in that range depends on the care your loved one needs. Your exact rate is set at the free consultation, after we understand the full picture.

**If WRHA already covers some hours, you only pay for the rest.** Say your loved one needs 3 hours every day (21 hours a week) and WRHA approves 10 hours a week. You'd only cover the other 11 hours privately: about $275 to $495 a week instead of $525 to $945.

## 5 ways Winnipeg families pay less

**1. Self and Family Managed Care (SFMC).** This WRHA program lets eligible home care clients and their families manage their own care with public funding, instead of having the region schedule staff. Eligibility is set by the health authority, so ask your case coordinator. Our [home care funding guide](/home-care-funding-manitoba.html) walks through how it works.

**2. Veterans Independence Program (VIP).** If your loved one served, Veterans Affairs Canada may help cover home care. For 2026, VIP home care services can be covered up to $13,779.63 a year, depending on eligibility. That's roughly $265 a week. [See VAC's 2026 rates](https://public.cdn.cloud.veterans.gc.ca/pdf/resources/rates/vip-max-rates-table-2026.pdf).

**3. Tax credits for attendant care.** Paying for attendant care can count as a medical expense on your tax return. If your loved one is approved for the Disability Tax Credit (Form T2201), the CRA allows up to $10,000 a year of attendant care wages to be claimed along with the disability amount ($20,000 in the year of death). Full-time attendant care at home has different rules, where you claim either the care or the disability amount. These rules are detailed, so talk to a tax professional. [CRA's medical expenses guide](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/rc4065/medical-expenses.html) has the details.

**4. Extended health or long-term care insurance.** Check old policies. Some long-term care or critical illness plans pay toward home care once certain conditions are met. Look for the benefit trigger, the daily or monthly maximum, and any waiting period.

**5. Mix public and private hours.** The most affordable plans usually combine both: WRHA for the hours it covers, private care for the gaps. You pay only for what public care doesn't reach.

## What drives the cost up or down

- **Hours.** The biggest factor by far. A few focused visits cost far less than full days.
- **Day versus overnight.** Overnight care is priced differently from daytime visits, so ask about it separately.
- **Level of care.** Specialized needs, like later-stage dementia, sit at the higher end of the range.
- **The same caregiver.** Consistency doesn't change the hourly rate, but it often means fewer wasted hours, because a familiar caregiver doesn't start from scratch every visit.

## An ER nurse's advice: plan before the crisis

As an ER nurse, I saw the same pattern again and again. An older parent comes in after a fall. We do the scan, and nothing is broken. They go home. The next time we see them, it's something more serious: a worse fall, or an infection because nobody was there to help them stay clean and dry after the first one. Now they're confused and in worse shape than when they first came in.

That's the most expensive mistake families make. They wait for the crisis. A few hours of help at home costs far less, in money and in health, than a second trip to the ER.

**Signs your family needs more hours, or overnight care:**

- You're using more and more of your work hours to handle emergencies.
- You can't focus at work, because you're waiting for the next call: "Mom fell again," or "She forgot her diabetes medication."
- The food you cook ahead of time comes back untouched.
- Showers and basic hygiene are being skipped.
- You're exhausted, and the people helping are starting to burn out.

**One free thing you can do this week:** give your loved one a schedule with people in it. Line up family, friends or neighbours to check in at set times that fit *their* day. If Mom wakes up at 10, someone stops by around then: "Good morning, how are you feeling? Want to go for a walk? Read the paper together? What should we make for breakfast?" Add regular outings, like a community centre program. Knowing who is coming and when gives them something to look forward to, and it tells them people care. It isn't only the illness that wears people down. Loneliness does too.

## Why the same caregiver matters for dementia

Think about why you've kept the same friends for years. Familiarity builds trust. When it's the same caregiver every time, your loved one knows who is coming and when. A stranger isn't walking into their home. That sense of security matters for anyone, and even more for someone living with dementia.
